# SMART BREVITY FOR GRC
### A personal framework for executive risk communication

Source concept: Smart Brevity (VandeHei, Allen, Schwartz, Axios). Adapted here for risk and compliance writing specifically, with real before/after examples from my own material.

---

## THE PRINCIPLES

**1. Bottom line first.** The first sentence is the conclusion, not the setup. If someone reads only that sentence, they should know what I think and what I want.

**2. Why it matters, stated, not implied.** Don't make the reader infer why they should care. Say it directly, in one sentence.

**3. One idea per artifact.** A risk report, a recommendation, a status update, each does one job. If I'm bundling three points into one message, split them or pick the one that matters most.

**4. Bullets over paragraphs.** Paragraphs hide the structure of my thinking. Bullets expose it. An executive should be able to scan, not read.

**5. Short sentences, active voice.** Cut qualifiers ("I think," "it's worth noting"). Cut throat-clearing. State the thing.

**6. "Go deeper" is optional, not default.** Offer the detail. Don't force it. Let the reader choose to go further.

**The test before sending anything:** if someone reads only the first line, do they already have what they need to act? If not, the first line is wrong, not the rest of the document.

## TEMPLATE 1: EXECUTIVE / RISK SUMMARY

**Structure:**
```
[Bottom line: one sentence, the conclusion]

Why it matters: [one sentence, the consequence or stakes]

What happened / what's true:
- [bullet]
- [bullet]
- [bullet]

Go deeper: [optional, one line, offer not obligation]
```

### Before (paragraph form, from my own material)

"I worked with a Chief Digital Security Services leader and CISO who never cared about risk, only vulnerabilities. It was hard to get a seat at the table. What changed it was building risk reports that mapped risk themes directly to the vulnerabilities he already tracked, structured as what, why, and the ask. I met him in his frame instead of arguing him into mine. That's what opened the door to talking about ownership and accountability around treatment planning, which is what he was actually missing."

### After (Smart Brevity)

**I got a vulnerability-only CISO to engage with risk by speaking his language first, not mine.**

Why it matters: influence without authority is the hardest GRC skill, and it's the one that actually moves a program forward.

What happened:
- He didn't care about risk, only vulnerabilities
- I built reports that mapped risk themes to the vulnerabilities he already tracked
- Each report stated what, why, and the ask
- That earned the seat at the table, and opened the door to ownership and accountability

Go deeper: happy to walk through a specific report if useful.

## TEMPLATE 2: RECOMMENDATION / PROPOSAL

**Structure:**
```
[Recommendation: one sentence, what I want them to do]

Why it matters: [one sentence, the cost of not doing it]

The ask:
- [specific, concrete item]
- [specific, concrete item]
- [specific, concrete item]

Go deeper: [optional detail, offered not forced]
```

### Before (paragraph form, from my own material)

"The tool surfaces the data, but leadership still has to decide on risk acceptances, anything flagged for acceptance needs a VP sign-off, same governed-acceptance principle as the policy exception ticket in the register, not a silent default. Leadership also needs to hold directors accountable to their own health scores and treatment velocity, the metric becomes the obligation, not just the report. They need to set and enforce risk appetite thresholds, the tool shows breaches, someone has to act on them. And they need to prioritize and resource remediation, I'd want a what-if view that shows the highest-leverage closures, paired with a gamification layer that makes burn-down visible and motivating across directors, so leadership can fund the highest-impact fix, not just acknowledge it exists."

### After (Smart Brevity)

**The tool tells you what is happening. Leadership decides what to do about it.**

Why it matters: a register without leadership action is just a list. These four decisions are what make it a program.

The ask:
- Sign off on risk acceptances at VP level, no silent defaults
- Hold directors accountable to their own health score and treatment velocity
- Set and enforce risk appetite, act on the breaches the tool shows
- Resource remediation, prioritized by highest-leverage closures

Go deeper: happy to walk through how the what-if view and gamification layer support the last point.

## TEMPLATE 3: WEEKLY STATUS UPDATE (PERSONA-BASED)

**Structure:**
```
Bottom line: [health score, one-word trend: up / down / flat]

Why it matters: [one sentence on what changed and what it means]

For [CFO / CISO / Director, pick the audience]:
- [the one or two metrics that audience cares about]

Action needed: [if any, one line. If none, say "none this week."]

Go deeper: [link to full register or dashboard view]
```

### Before (paragraph form, hypothetical as currently drafted)

"This week the overall risk health score moved slightly, reflecting continued progress on treatment of high and critical risks, though two new findings were identified through the vendor assessment process that will need to be triaged and assigned an owner. Treatment velocity remains roughly in line with last month's pace, and there were no risks that breached SLA and were auto-accepted this period, which is a positive sign of engagement across the teams currently being tracked."

### After (Smart Brevity)

**Bottom line: health score 68, up 3 points from last week.**

Why it matters: treatment is keeping pace with new findings, no SLA breaches this week.

For the CISO: zero risks auto-accepted this period, full engagement across tracked teams.

For Directors: two new vendor-assessment findings need an owner assigned by Friday.

Action needed: assign owners on the two new findings.

Go deeper: full register here [link].

## QUICK SELF-CHECK BEFORE SENDING ANYTHING

- [ ] Does the first sentence stand alone as the conclusion?
- [ ] Is "why it matters" stated, not assumed?
- [ ] Is this one idea, not three bundled together?
- [ ] Could this be bullets instead of a paragraph?
- [ ] Did I cut every "I think," "it's worth noting," "just to add"?
- [ ] Is the detail offered as optional, not forced into the main message?

If I fail more than one of these, rewrite before sending.
